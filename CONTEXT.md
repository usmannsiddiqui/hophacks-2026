# CONTEXT — Mashwara glossary

Domain glossary for this codebase. Code, tests, ADRs and conversation use these words
with these meanings. Add a term here in the same commit that introduces it.

---

## People

**Patient.** The person at the counter. Speaks Urdu (Hindi fallback, ADR 0003). Never
touches the screen; hears the app in her language. Our demo patient is **Nani**.

**Counter operator.** Whoever holds the tablet behind the counter — the shopkeeper or a
**volunteer**. Speaks English. Reads the live transcript, asks follow-ups out loud, holds
the sale when a flag appears, sends the case. Types nothing.

**Volunteer.** A counter operator who is giving time rather than working the shop. Same
screen, same role. One of the two giving mechanisms in the philanthropic claim.

**Pharmacist.** A qualified, remote, pro bono pharmacist. Opens cases from a queue,
reviews, writes advice, approves. Asynchronous — minutes to hours (ADR 0004). The other
giving mechanism.

## The case

**Case.** One patient visit. Created when the counter operator presses Start; closed when
the pharmacist approves. Shape in `docs/specs/contracts.md`. Lives in the Neon `cases`
table (ADR 0005). Starts from zero — no chart, no prior history (ADR 0002).

**Transcript.** The ordered list of utterances in the case. Each utterance keeps the
original-language text and the English text side by side (ADR 0003). Speakers:
`patient`, `counter`, `app`.

**Med item.** One thing the patient is asking for or already takes. Always carries a
**term** (from the closed vocabulary), **her words** (the exact original-language phrase
that produced it), a **source** (`voice` or `photo`) and a **role**.

**Role.** `requested` — what she came to buy. `takes` — a medicine she already uses.
`remedy` — a home/herbal/hakeem remedy. Roles are how the bubble map colors nothing and
labels everything.

**Provenance pair.** The clinical term and her words, rendered together, always. A UI
invariant: a med item never appears without the words that produced it.

**Unrecognised.** Gemini could not map her words to a vocabulary term. Not an error —
it sends the app back to ask a follow-up. Renders as a dashed card.

## Safety vocabulary

**Closed vocabulary.** ~150 terms: common Pakistani OTC brands and generics, chronic
meds, and common desi remedies. Gemini may only output a term from this list or
`unrecognised`. Owned by stream A. File: `data/substances.json` (`substances[]`).

**Interaction table.** ~40 rows of `{ termA, termB, severity, reason, source }`. The only
thing that can produce a flag (ADR 0001). Every row has a citable source. File:
`data/substances.json` (`interactions[]`).

**Flag.** A row from the interaction table that matches two med items in the case.
Carries severity (`high` | `moderate`), the reason and the source. Rendered in the `flag`
color. Never generated by an LLM.

**Question.** A gap the LLM noticed that the table does not cover, phrased as a question
for the pharmacist ("Worth asking whether she takes X?"). Rendered interrogative, in the
`ask` color, with a `?` glyph. Never a statement, never a flag (ADR 0001).

**Hold.** The moment a flag appears on the counter screen: the sale pauses until the
pharmacist responds. The physical beat of the demo.

**Severity.** `high` or `moderate`. Sets bubble size on the map and the label on a flag.

## Advice

**Advice.** The pharmacist's free-text plan plus a per-item **verdict**.

**Verdict.** `keep` | `stop` | `swap`, one per med item. `stop` is the only colored chip.

**Readback.** The advice spoken to the patient in her language via TTS. Step 10.

## Screens

**Counter screen.** Tablet, landscape. Left: conversation. Right: the case building.
States: idle → listening → flag → sent → advice.

**Pharmacist console.** Desktop. Queue sidebar; case view with transcript, **bubble map**
and action column. States: queue → case open → approved.

**Bubble map.** Nodes = med items; size = worst severity touching the node; fill = status
color or neutral; edges = interaction rows. Hover shows her words.

## Design words

**Status tokens.** `clear`, `ask`, `flag`. The only saturated colors in the product.
"The only color on screen is a flag" (ADR 0006).

**Nastaliq.** Noto Nastaliq Urdu, the typeface for every patient-facing string. RTL,
line-height ~1.9. Urdu never falls back to a Latin face.
